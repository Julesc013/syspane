# W-25 persistent controller and automatic GNOME reattachment

Run a new bounded `--controller-recovery live|no-reattach|revoke` family in the
existing private GNOME/Xvfb laboratory. Keep the earlier shell-owned-session
experiments and their exact oracles unchanged. This composition makes the native
controller the POSIX session/group leader. Its collector, shell and replacement
shell inherit that one session; no IPC authentication rule is relaxed.

## Ownership and authority

Extend the existing CollectorProbe continuity composition with a `desktop` mode.
It owns the continuous real worker, RestartGate, current consumer policy, Sessions
and exact shell child handles. Launch a self child which arms parent-death protection
and replaces itself with the pinned GNOME ELF through the platform adapter. Hold
the executable open, reject noncanonical/symlink/nonregular/set-ID inputs, preserve
PID/session and the parent-death signal through exec, and close inherited nonstdio
descriptors. The existing Child owner remains sole reaper; confirmed native wait
is the only stop proof. A termination request does not establish exit.

The harness provides the pinned shell path and isolated desktop environment. It
cannot ask this native mode to terminate or attach to a user shell. The independent
observer holds controller, collector, shell and DING lifetimes before faults. Only
those owned handles may be signalled. The controller never grants itself signing,
installation, protected-policy, user-session or release authority.

The initial shell is explicitly activated once after independent glyph calibration.
After a shell fault the controller confirms old exit, observes the existing
one-second first backoff, then launches its identical pinned shell automatically.
The replacement receives the current connection/parent identity and scene-active
decision through its inherited private launch environment. The extension attaches
automatically to the controller's authenticated socket and imports a complete
original snapshot through the existing native NetworkView. The observer must not
call Start, inject values or enable the operational tile on the replacement.

Extend the existing asynchronous session owner with an attached-peer form. It owns
its socket, native view, callbacks and bounded queues; it never owns or signals the
external controller process. Preserve two-second handshake startup, 50 ms ticks,
one-second heartbeats and three-second consumer/source leases. Its close path must
cancel pending writes within one second even without an owned child. Keep the old
subprocess-backed network/watch behavior and limits unchanged.

The controller remains independently authorized to collect when the typed desktop
grant is revoked. In `revoke`, apply revision 8 before any replacement admission;
no later shell or operational delivery is authorized. Global collection revocation,
installed policy and a full user-session manager remain separate contracts.

## Bounds and failure behavior

The new native desktop controller has a 60-second bound and its real worker a
90-second bound; the earlier fourteen/twenty-five-second experiment is unchanged.
Allow at most fifteen seconds for each GNOME connection startup, retaining the
ordinary protocol handshake and lease deadlines once connected. Keep source
service running during startup, confirmed-exit waiting and 1/2/4-second retries.
Retain the maximum three replacements, quarantine and circuit rules. The outer
laboratory attempt is bounded to 70 seconds and one injected shell crash.

On explicit owner termination, stop demand and request termination of only the
held shell, with a two-second native exit bound and exact-child force fallback.
Stop the source through its existing health shutdown and confirm its exit. The
laboratory retains/reaps owned descendants and checks its private group is empty.
No persistent endpoint or detached helper may survive final cleanup.

Keep one original full snapshot for fanout, existing model/framing/queue budgets,
4 MiB private source and delivery journals, 16 MiB private pixel evidence and

private. A missing laboratory or failed candidate remains unqualified.
